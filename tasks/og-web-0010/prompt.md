# Courtroom Clue Trial

Build a complete browser courtroom deduction game, **The Warehouse Trial**, with a title, case brief, interactive testimony, evidence inspection, objections, penalties and meaningful verdicts. Deliver a self-contained `index.html` package using local HTML, CSS, JavaScript and assets. A courtroom art atlas is supplied; you may use it or create a coherent replacement. The game must run from a local HTTP server without external services.

- **R1 — Establish the case.** Normal startup opens a styled case-file title screen. Opening the case shows a brief: Mara Bell is accused of stealing dockworkers' payroll from a warehouse safe at 20:00; Elias Vale claims he saw her. Introduce the defense, judge, witness and prosecutor. The brief explains the accusation, location, evidence and how to inspect and present exhibits before testimony begins.
- **R2 — Relevant evidence.** Provide at least 4 designed, inspectable exhibits: `outage` establishes municipal power was off from 19:30 to 21:00 and the ceiling lights have no backup; `fingerprint` establishes Vale's fresh print inside the safe; `ticket` places Mara on a ferry at 18:00 but does not establish her location at 20:00; `key` includes a warehouse plan showing the safe 6 meters inside, unreachable from the doorway. Show readable facts and a persistent selected state. Each exhibit has a distinct image and logical role.
- **R3 — Interactive deduction.** Each of 2 testimony layers has at least 3 statements, previous/next navigation, a visible statement index and a press action that reveals additional relevant information without costing credibility. In the first layer, statement 2 claims an identification under municipal ceiling lights at 20:00; pressing rules out lanterns or backup lighting. Only `outage` against this statement establishes the first contradiction. It triggers an objection and a changed, playable second layer. There, statement 2 denies touching the safe interior and statement 3 denies reaching beyond the doorway. Presenting `fingerprint` against statement 2 or `key` against statement 3 establishes the second contradiction and the successful verdict. Other statements provide context; one exhibit must not work against every statement.
- **R4 — Stakes and closure.** Start with credibility 3. A wrong exhibit/statement pair costs 1 and visibly provokes the judge. Presenting with no exhibit selected costs nothing. At 0, enter a case-specific mistrial. Successful and failed verdicts show contradictions established, penalties and remaining credibility, and offer retry and title navigation. Final states reject further testimony actions. Retry restores the first layer, full credibility and no selected exhibit; title navigation starts a fresh case.
- **R5 — Presentation.** Stage the courtroom roles with illustrated portraits; at least 2 characters visibly react to objections, pressing or penalties. Use authored case-file/evidence art, coherent typography and themed UI. Animate objections, reactions, screen transitions and verdict feedback. Keep statements, details, credibility, evidence and actions readable without overlap at 1280 by 720; a 390 by 844 layout may scroll. A flat text form with decorative labels does not satisfy the presentation requirement.
- **R6 — Browser interaction contract.** Expose visible controls `#start`, `#begin`, `#prev`, `#next`, `#press`, `#present`, `#reset` and `#title` where appropriate. Evidence controls are `#e-outage`, `#e-fingerprint`, `#e-ticket`, `#e-key`. Display `#witness`, `#statement`, `#detail`, `#stage`, `#index`, `#cred`, `#fact`, `#feedback`, and verdict `#status`, `#objections`, `#penalties`. Use real buttons and visible artwork. Support deterministic query scenarios `?scenario=testimony`, `?scenario=second-layer`, and `?scenario=near-mistrial`; these start directly in the respective decision state, with the last starting at credibility 1.
- **R7 — Replay evidence.** Include 1–10 JSON input traces in `demo_outputs/`, covering a correct deduction chain and a wrong accusation across the set. Each trace has optional `scenario`, `duration_frames` up to 600, and ordered `events` with `frame`, `type` and input fields. Playback uses 30 frames per second at 1280 by 720. Supported types are `mouse_click`, `mouse_down`, `mouse_up`, `mouse_move`, `key_press`, `key_down`, `key_up`, `wait`; pointer fields are `x`, `y`, `button`, and keyboard field is `keycode`. Traces must use actual input and produce the same outcome on fresh launches.
- **R8 — Acceptance and scoring.** Automated checks cover launch, navigation, evidence facts, statement-specific deductions, both layers, penalties, final-state guards, retry and input replays. Full assessment also retains content, visual readability and artistic presentation: mechanics 15%, content depth 35%, functional visuals 15%, presentation/art 35%, gated by successful launch. Use `rubric.md` for individual criteria. Automated functional success does not award unreviewed artistic or narrative-quality points.

List trace paths in the root `demos.json` array. The replay set must reach both successful verdict and mistrial, so both complete endings can be reviewed.

Create the complete game, not just a test screen. Keep the required deduction facts and browser controls; wording, layout, animation and artistic treatment remain your design choices. Task input is this English prompt; the Chinese companion is a translation only.
